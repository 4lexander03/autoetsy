## Honest risks

1. **We would be entering directly behind CypherBlake.** The mechanic is a generic puzzle
   form, not their IP, and the category supports multiple $11k+ shops — but we must
   differentiate on difficulty tiers, theme breadth, design quality, and bundle structure
   rather than cloning. **Kill risk if we merely imitate.**
2. **Trend vs. fad.** 400–1000% growth rates could be a spike. True crime is a durable
   cultural genre, which argues trend — but this is the assumption most likely to be wrong.
3. **Generated ≠ fun.** Puzzle *validity* is machine-checkable; puzzle *enjoyment* is not.
   Difficulty calibration and clue elegance are the real engineering problems, and bad
   output means bad reviews fast. Mitigation: solver-derived difficulty metrics
   (deduction steps required, branching factor) plus human playtesting of the first batch.
4. **It does not use the existing rib-slicer codebase.** See below.

## What this means for the rib-slicer engine

Following the evidence costs us the existing asset. Stated plainly:

- **Digital soundwave art is a dead market** — 1,120 listings, top listing **$72/month**,
  nearly all listings at $0. Discarded on hard filter.
- **The value in that category is entirely personalized + physical** — 3,646 listings,
  top $3,760/mo, prices $30–175, and new entrants (ArtsyVoiceprint, 3 months, $2,678/mo)
  are winning. **The seller's existing plan to sell physical built pieces is validated by
  this data.** It is simply a different business from the automated digital shop.
- Laser-cut SVG (65.5) and STL (65.5) — the two digital routes the engine could serve —
  both lose to A on competition quality and price integrity.

**Recommendation: keep rib-slicer as the separate physical/personalized business it
already is, and do not bend the automated shop toward it.** Criterion 7 gave it every
fair advantage and it still finished 18 points behind.
